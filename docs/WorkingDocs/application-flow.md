# Application Flow

This page records current 05 behavior and its source owners. The [transition overview](Winemaker04_to_05_Transition_Overview.md) and [design record](design.md) describe product direction; they do not establish runtime behavior. The active [core-game transition plan](../../plans/2026-11-21-core-game-transition.md) is the evidence register for the integrated test, PostgreSQL, and browser gates.

The source now contains a server-authoritative core-game implementation from vineyard establishment through harvest, wine processing and maturation, cellar assessment, and commercial commands. The screens and domain paths are in place; the active [core-game transition plan](../../plans/2026-11-21-core-game-transition.md) records current integration verification. The previous prototype-only status is obsolete. Game-balance constants and catalog values described here are provisional implementation choices under the core-loop task, not newly approved design formulas.

## State owners and boundaries

- [CompanyState](../../src/game/core/CompanyState.ts) composes the calendar, elapsed week and deterministic ID sequence with domain-owned estate, winery, inventory, equipment, finance, work, and commercial state. [SnapshotValidation](../../src/game/core/SnapshotValidation.ts) validates the current exact shape and cross-domain invariants; it does not migrate older snapshots.
- [Vineyard rules and catalog](../../src/game/vineyard/) own sites, grape varieties, weather response, crop growth, ripeness, harvestable quantity, rot, and immutable harvest origin. The current prototype offers four grape varieties and three sites. [Origin](../../src/game/vineyard/Origin.ts) keeps agricultural Terroir Potential separate from commercial Origin Prestige and land asset value.
- [Wine types and operations](../../src/game/wine/) own each physical lot's origin, immutable harvest anchors, material form, concentrations, process settings, lineage, and history. They do not own a second copy of quantity or placement.
- [Inventory](../../src/game/inventory/) owns each live lot's positive integer quantity, unit, location, vessel allocation, and bottle reservations. Units are grams of grapes, millilitres of liquid, or whole 750 ml bottles. A physical vessel contains one coherent live liquid lot; splitting or blending creates an explicit new state rather than silently combining independent lots.
- [Equipment](../../src/game/equipment/) owns vessel material, capacity, condition, fill count, and cellar temperature. It resolves transient wine environments from the lot's current holding and vessel; the environment is not duplicated on the lot or vessel.
- [Work](../../src/game/work/) owns staff capacity and harvest activities. The initial company state has starter workers, but full recruitment, training, teams, and people progression are outside this loop.
- [Finance](../../src/game/finance/) owns integer-cent cash, postings, wage arrears, and basic processing costs. Vineyard/equipment purchases, pressing, bottling, sales, contract delivery, and weekly wages compose finance results into the same company candidate.
- [Commercial types and commands](../../src/game/commercial/types.ts) and [commercial actions](../../src/game/commands/CommercialActions.ts) define NPC buyers, wine contracts, reservations, sales, and fulfillment commands. The active plan records the current implementation and integration evidence for pricing, eligibility, and due-contract outcomes; this page does not infer those details from Winemaker04.
- [Command routing](../../src/game/core/Commands.ts) strictly reads current command payloads, and [decideCommand](../../src/game/core/decideCommand.ts) routes them to domain actions or the time transition. Domain rules return candidate state without browser, database, or React access.
- [CompanyView](../../src/game/queries/CompanyView.ts) derives the display model, assessments, quotes, summaries, and current-condition forecasts. [ActionPreviews](../../src/game/queries/ActionPreviews.ts) calls the same pure command decision as execution and discards the candidate. The game panels under [src/components/game](../../src/components/game/) render this view and submit commands; they do not own simulation calculations.

## Player and company access

The browser connects the React/Pixi application shell to the company authority. [CompanySession](../../src/authority/session.ts) coordinates commands, revisions, receipts, and committed snapshots. [CompanySessions](../../src/server/companySessions.ts) owns HTTP-host session lifetime. The [HTTP authority](../../src/server/api/server.ts) scopes requests to an existing selected database profile or the shared unassigned-company scope; the [PostgreSQL save adapter](../../src/server/postgres/PostgresCompanySavePort.ts) commits company snapshots, revisions, command receipts, and recovery records in transactions. Database credentials and owner-scope binding remain server-only.

Named player profiles are database records. The welcome screen lists profiles from the authority; creating or renaming a profile updates its database row. Selecting a profile sets its ID as company owner, so that profile can be selected from another browser. On a normal return the last selection is restored; when there is no saved selection and only one profile exists, it is selected automatically. Logging out returns to selection. The screen also offers company creation without a profile; these unassigned companies can be listed across browsers.

Profiles and unassigned companies have no password or private authentication boundary: anyone who can use the service can select them. Profile account linking and recovery are not implemented. Supabase Auth is not used. These are selectable names and company scopes, not authenticated accounts.

The browser creates stable IDs for player/company creation requests and retains each request until confirmed. Retrying player creation uses the same ID and name; reusing an ID with a different name is rejected. A company creation retry confirms the same scope, ID, and name before opening it. A confirmed ID conflict clears the saved request and reports the conflict; an unknown result stays pending. Web Locks keep tabs from replacing the same retained creation request. Malformed saved creation records can be explicitly discarded, but are not silently overwritten.

The browser stores a command intent before sending it and uses Web Locks to coordinate commands, recovery, and company switching across same-origin tabs. A command uses a stable command ID and expected company revision. The server receipt prevents an accepted command from applying twice; stale revisions are rejected. If an outcome is uncertain, the client preserves and retries the same intent or resolves a server-known reservation before allowing another command or a company switch. An intent lost before server reservation can only be retried from the original browser, which retained its ID and payload; another device can resolve a server-known reservation but cannot recover an unsent intent. A valid unresolved intent blocks other commands and company switching. An unreadable local record remains preserved and keeps that company read-only. Recovery can resolve an independently valid server or retained command ID; only a confirmed matching outcome clears that record. The player can copy the unreadable record and leave the company after a fresh authority load confirms there is no server-known pending attempt. After a command or recovery, the client reloads the committed snapshot; if that refresh fails, advancing stays disabled until a successful refresh. A cross-tab settlement also requires refresh. Local storage and Web Locks must be available. The HTTP host releases completed idle sessions, but retains sessions with unresolved recovery or required-refresh state; durable reservations remain visible to other server instances. These rules preserve the original valid-intent recovery boundary while allowing safe inspection and exit when local storage is damaged.

## Company actions and weekly flow

The player starts a new company from the current state shape, establishes a vineyard, advances time one week at a time, and assigns starter staff to a harvest. Harvest work can progress partially and reports blockers such as immature grapes, heavy rain, insufficient receiving capacity, unavailable staff, or unpaid wages. Workers share their available time evenly among productive assigned activities; blocked or capped activities release unused time for another fair-share round. Priority and stable IDs resolve contested crop and storage; only feasible output becomes harvested material.

The company state transition in [AdvanceTime](../../src/game/simulation/AdvanceTime.ts) produces one pure candidate. Each weekly advance plans the calendar boundary and a reproducible weather sample, advances existing vineyards, computes harvest-work progress, and develops every already stored live lot for seven daily steps. The daily engine resolves the current environment from each lot, holding, vessel, and cellar temperature before running wine development and risks. It then applies feasible harvest output at the week boundary, creating grape lots with captured origin and anchors. Those new lots do not also develop during the week in which they are harvested. The transition applies vessel wear, pays weekly wages as far as cash allows, carries any remaining wage arrears, advances contract state, and validates the complete candidate before returning it. The authority commits the whole candidate atomically; the browser publishes only the committed snapshot.

The order and current constants are implementation facts, but their numerical balance is provisional. Vineyard prices, yields, weather/growth responses, worker output, salaries, vessel effects, process yields, sensory coefficients, target profiles, and risk rates are tunable prototype values. The approved direction is the causal separation of inherited potential, physical development, sensory output, contextual evaluation, and commercial value; these current equations do not replace or extend the approved [design record](design.md).

## Vineyard, harvest, and wine development

The prototype catalogs Pinot Noir, Tempranillo, Sauvignon Blanc, and Chardonnay, with three authored sites. Site/grape suitability and current vine condition contribute to Terroir Potential; Origin Prestige is a separate site reputation used by commercial rules. Weekly weather and crop rules advance condition, crop size, ripeness, and harvest-time rot. Harvest creates an immutable vintage/origin snapshot and Raw Material Anchors. Inventory records the harvested grams separately from the wine lot's physical concentrations and lineage.

The persisted material forms are grapes, must, wine, and bottled. Active fermentation and its settings are explicit process facts. Readiness and operating labels are derived from material form, physical state, active processes, faults, and maturity rather than copied from a Winemaker04 lifecycle enum; the cellar exposes current bottling readiness with blocker reasons. Inventory separately owns every quantity and placement.

Pressing is an explicit action. Intensity, destemming, and skin contact trade juice yield against finite phenolic extraction and exposure. The resulting must occupies one maintained vessel with enough capacity. Fermentation settings establish temperature and contact choices. Daily development converts available sugar to alcohol, may stall under poor actual temperatures, and applies extraction, maturation, and risk processes in deterministic order. Current vessel material, fill/headspace, vessel condition and use history, cellar temperature, and bottled storage determine the environment. Steel, oak, and concrete therefore affect development through different provisional exposure rules.

Transfer, split, blend, bottle, discard, and sale are explicit inventory transactions. A liquid lot cannot share a vessel with another independent lot. Splits preserve the source lineage on each portion; blends create a new wine identity with source fractions and references to parent lots. Earlier histories remain with those archived parents, so reblending split siblings does not duplicate shared events. Bottling consumes liquid quantity, creates a bottle holding, and stores a historical bottling assessment. Wine continues to age after bottling and its maturity outlook can later decline. Consumed lots are archived with their history instead of remaining active development inputs.

## Sensory output and assessment

[Sensory projections](../../src/game/evaluation/Sensory.ts) and [taste projections](../../src/game/evaluation/Taste.ts) derive current structure, taste families, and tasting descriptors from the lot's current physical state and its inherited source context. [Profiles](../../src/game/evaluation/Profiles.ts) create bounded, contextual moving targets from grape identity and anchor capacity. [MovingTargets](../../src/game/evaluation/MovingTargets.ts) scores a profile's exact axes against those targets. [Assessment](../../src/game/evaluation/Assessment.ts) composes separate structural fit, taste fit, descriptors, and physical-fault diagnoses.

The current provisional quality policy starts from structural coherence and applies one reduction based on the most severe current physical fault. Taste fit remains a separate style and buyer-context output. Physical faults such as oxidation, spoilage, green character, and stuck fermentation are kept distinct from ordinary structural imbalance; a poor structure score alone is not a physical fault. These mappings, target widths, weights, scoring curve, and the maximum fault reduction are provisional tuning choices.

The cellar shows identity, source, quantity and reservations, current physical values, structure/taste axes and target ranges, descriptors, fault explanations, bottling snapshot, and lot history. Its forecast advances a copy for 12 weeks using current process, vessel, and cellar conditions. It is a scenario under those conditions, not a guaranteed drinking window; previews and forecasts do not mutate company state.

## Commercial and financial scope

The commercial domain supplies three company-local NPC buyers: a local merchant, a restaurant, and a collector. It generates deterministic offers in week 1 and every fourth elapsed week when that buyer has no open or accepted contract. Offers have a fixed 16-week delivery window; overdue offers expire and accepted contracts default, with one relationship penalty at the transition. Contract eligibility checks bottle form, quantity, delivery week, current structure and taste fit, age, and any required grape across all blend sources. The current offers use no shared-market listing or settlement.

Direct bottle quotes combine the current quality assessment with buyer-specific taste fit, maturity preference, source-fraction-weighted Origin Prestige, and buyer relationship. A severe oxidation or spoilage fault can make a lot ineligible for direct sale or contract delivery. Contract delivery requires the full bottle target to be reserved and currently eligible. Reservations reduce quantity available to other actions, can be released, and are consumed when a contract is fulfilled. Direct sale relationship gain scales with bottles sold; contract goodwill uses the contract target only once even when delivered from multiple lots. A sale or delivery consumes inventory, appends sale history, and posts the associated revenue in the same candidate. Stable command IDs, receipts, and company revisions protect these actions against duplicate retries and stale writes.

The buyer profiles, price coefficients, contract terms, maturity preferences, and relationship adjustments are provisional game-balance values. Only the basic company cash ledger, processing costs, weekly wages, and wage arrears are included in this core loop. Full recruitment and training, research and grants, lending, buildings, shared-market settlement, multiplayer trade, prestige/achievement progression, and online leaderboards remain outside its scope.
