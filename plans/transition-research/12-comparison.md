# Topic 12 comparison: company-local wine sales and contracts

Approved 05 direction preserves sales, pricing, customers and contracts while separating agricultural Terroir Potential from commercial Origin Prestige ([design.md](../../docs/WorkingDocs/design.md); [transition overview](../../docs/WorkingDocs/Winemaker04_to_05_Transition_Overview.md)). This slice is company-local NPC trade; shared-market authority, peer settlement and full finance systems stay deferred ([transition-contracts.md](../transition-contracts.md), Scope).

Industri Clicker offers the clearest order-economy comparison. [salesOrders.ts](C:/GitHub/industriClicker/game/sales/salesOrders.ts#L263) derives bids from customer type, relationship, prestige, purchasing power and economy phase, then stores reference price, bid, quality multiplier, reward and expiry on the offer. Its [tests](C:/GitHub/industriClicker/tests/sales/salesOrders.test.ts#L90) check fixed bid terms and that offer-time quality is frozen into reward; its expiry path adjusts customer relationship once when removing an order ([salesOrders.ts](C:/GitHub/industriClicker/game/sales/salesOrders.ts#L445)). This is a useful lifecycle pattern, not a wine formula. Industri's [qualityMath.ts](C:/GitHub/industriClicker/game/quality/qualityMath.ts#L77) reduces inputs to a quantity-weighted scalar Q bounded by input, research, facility, production and staff ceilings. 05 instead exposes structure, taste, faults and one provisional quality in [Assessment.ts](../../src/game/evaluation/Assessment.ts) and [types.ts](../../src/game/evaluation/types.ts); buyer/style fit should remain explicit rather than treating industrial Q as wine assessment.

Industri's [finance.ts](C:/GitHub/industriClicker/game/finance/finance.ts#L176) updates balance and dated ledger entry through one operation. TraderGame01's [purchaseMarketListing](C:/GitHub/tradergame01/src/lib/firebase/marketService.ts#L428) writes buyer/seller balances, stock and listing in one Firestore transaction, but its reservation query is outside the transaction read set and asynchronous audit writes are initiated inside the retryable callback. Its contract tick also launches sorted transfers concurrently before a later progress checkpoint ([contractService.ts](C:/GitHub/tradergame01/src/lib/firebase/contractService.ts#L318)). Reuse the atomicity goal, not those multiplayer paths. TraderGame04's [marketService.ts](C:/GitHub/tradergame04/src/lib/market/marketService.ts#L79) integrates an inverse-supply curve over quantity; this commodity-market option does not establish wine demand or require quantity-sensitive NPC quotes.

**Proposed take and critical tests:** The planned seam is `createContractOffers(week)`, `advanceContracts(state, week)`, `acceptContract(contract, week)`, `quoteWine(lot, assessment, buyerId, relationship, bottles)`, `contractEligibility(contract, lot, assessment, bottles, week)` and `recordSale(state, sale)` in [transition-contracts.md](../transition-contracts.md); `src/game/commercial/Rules.ts` is absent in this checkout. Derive quotes from current lot assessment, buyer context and relationship; calculate blend Origin Prestige once from each [PhysicalLot.sources](../../src/game/wine/types.ts) fraction. Freeze generated contract price, but recheck current structure/taste/age eligibility on reservation and delivery. Keep reservations conserved and compose stock consumption, cash posting, sale record, lot archive and fulfilled status in one candidate command, protected by existing command receipts. Advance offer expiry, accepted-contract default and relationship effects deterministically once per week. Add focused cases in `tests/game/commercial.test.ts` for repeated same-week offers, price stability, threshold crossings, multi-source prestige weighting and double-count prevention; extend `tests/game/core-loop.test.ts`, `tests/game/inventory.test.ts`, `tests/api/game-loop.test.ts` and `tests/game/time-advance.test.ts` for reallocation/release, over-reservation, failed settlement preserving all state, duplicate-command replay and one-time expiry/default effects.
